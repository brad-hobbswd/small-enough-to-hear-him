Chapter Three

The Danger of Wanting a Name

A reflection on humility, recognition, and remaining centered upon Jesus.

Many people never begin with wrong intentions. A desire to be used by God often starts with sincerity, passion, and a genuine hunger to serve. A heart can begin with purity and still slowly drift toward something different without recognizing the change.

Recognition has a subtle voice.

A longing to serve can quietly become a longing to be seen. A desire to help can slowly become a desire for validation. Faithfulness can begin turning into performance when hearts become more aware of people watching than Jesus leading.

“The world asks, ‘How many people know your name?’ Jesus asks, ‘Do you know Mine?’”

Human nature often reaches for names, titles, positions, and visibility because recognition feels rewarding. Applause creates temporary satisfaction. Attention creates temporary excitement. People naturally enjoy being noticed.

Yet the danger begins when identity starts feeding from what only Jesus was meant to provide.

Scripture repeatedly points toward a kingdom pattern that looks different from human ambition. Many who appeared great in the eyes of society were overlooked by heaven. Many overlooked by society became vessels powerfully used by God.

A shepherd boy stood in a field while others looked toward stronger candidates. A fisherman followed Jesus without status or influence. Ordinary people walked beside Jesus and carried extraordinary purpose.

God has always looked beyond appearance. God sees hearts.

Recognition itself is not evil. Influence itself is not wrong. Platforms are not the problem. The condition of a heart determines whether those things become tools or distractions.

A willing vessel must guard against becoming more excited about opportunities than obedience.

A dangerous moment arrives whenever hearts begin saying, “Lord, make my name known.”

A healthier prayer says, “Lord, keep me close.”

John understood this tension deeply. His words carried both wisdom and surrender:

“He must increase, but I must decrease.” John 3:30

Those words do not sound natural to human ambition. Human ambition often says, “Increase me.” Human pride says, “Notice me.” Human insecurity says, “Validate me.”

Jesus invites hearts toward another way.

Decrease. Less striving. Less comparison. Less performance. More Jesus.

Many people fear becoming smaller because culture teaches that smaller means less important. Heaven reveals another reality. Smaller often means closer to dependence. Smaller often means greater trust. Smaller often creates room for Jesus to become larger within a life.

No big name. Just a willing vessel.

Small enough to hear Him means becoming content when heaven knows a name even while earth forgets one.
